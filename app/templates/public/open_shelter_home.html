At a Glance

A community-based care programme in Bhopal.

GSSKS describes its Open Shelter Homes as day-long care and support centres for children in need of care and protection (CNCP), including children and adolescents aged 6–17. The organisation profile identifies four units in the Gas-Affected Area (Chhola) and Pushpa Nagar, near Bhopal Railway Station.

Why Open Shelter Support Matters

A safe place when children and families need support.

The organisation profile identifies children in vulnerable circumstances, including children involved in begging or rag-picking, working children, children found in vulnerable settlements or on railway platforms, and children whose parents work as daily-wage labourers with uncertain working hours.

01

Children in vulnerable situations

The profile identifies children who may be exposed to unsafe streets, work, begging, rag-picking or other difficult circumstances.

02

Support for working families

Parents engaged in daily-wage work may have unpredictable return times. The brochure describes the shelter as a place where children's safety and care can be supported during the day.

03

Care with dignity

The programme description combines basic needs, learning, health, emotional support, recreation and family reintegration rather than focusing on shelter alone.

Programme Framework

Operating within the child-protection framework.

The supplied profile states that GSSKS's Open Shelter Home work is carried out under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 and Central Rules, 2016, within the Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS), now under Mission Vatsalya. It identifies the Department of Women and Child Development, Government of Madhya Pradesh, as the supervising department.

Facilities & Services

Care that supports everyday life and development.

The brochure and annual-report pages describe a mix of practical care, education, health, emotional support, recreation and rehabilitation follow-up.

Safe shelter & accommodation

A secure environment with age-appropriate arrangements. The document lists fire-safety measures, television, refrigerator, water purifiers and kitchen essentials among the facilities.

Nutrition & daily essentials

Balanced breakfast, lunch and dinner are described, alongside tea and snacks, clothing, footwear, toiletries such as soap and oil, and essential textbooks and study materials.

Education & learning support

Government-school admission support, basic literacy, non-formal bridge education, remedial classes, tuition or study assistance, textbooks and digital/computer learning.

Health & physical wellbeing

Routine medical check-ups, immunisation, first-aid support, physical fitness, sports and health-related guidance. The annual reports record medical check-ups and first-aid kits.

Counselling & life skills

Individual and group counselling, emotional support, trauma care, behaviour management, value education, anger and stress management, and life-skills learning.

Juvenile justice support

Assistance with Child Welfare Committee (CWC) and Juvenile Justice Board (JJB) procedures, Individual Care Plans (ICPs), inspections and rehabilitation follow-up.

Skill & vocational development

For adolescents, particularly those aged 14–17, the profile lists basic computer training, crafts or trade skills, career guidance and pre-employment training.

Recreation & personality development

Indoor and outdoor games, sports, arts, music, cultural activities, plantation, festivals, national days, birthdays, picnics and creative activities.

Family & social reintegration

Family counselling, restoration efforts and follow-up after restoration are included in the programme description.

Outreach, awareness & monitoring

Field visits to identify children in need, child-rights awareness, case files, progress reports and regular reporting to the Department of Women and Child Development.

Capacity & Reporting Context

Figures must be read alongside the year they describe.

The PDF contains different capacity figures in different reporting contexts. They are not interchangeable and are not presented here as current occupancy.

2022–23 REPORT269

Combined capacity stated for two units

The report prints Unit 1 capacity as 199 and Unit 2 capacity as 71, totalling 269. This is reproduced as a historical report figure and requires confirmation against the original records because it differs materially from the later four-unit capacity.

2023–24 REPORT100

Combined capacity stated for four units

The report states 25 places per unit across four units (4 × 25 = 100). It also describes the State's recognition of the operation of four Open Shelter Home units.

ORGANISATION BROCHURE~1,200

Children reportedly integrated over several years

The brochure states that approximately 1,200 children have been integrated into the social mainstream over several years. It does not specify the reporting period, unique-child methodology or independent verification.

Important interpretation269 and 100 are historical capacity figures printed in different annual-report contexts, not the number of children currently living at the centres. Approximately 1,200 is an organisation-reported cumulative figure, not an annual total. GSSKS should confirm the current sanctioned capacity, operating status of each unit and the cumulative-reach methodology before using these figures in funding or statutory claims.
Programme Commitment

Protection, care and opportunity—one child at a time.

Across the supplied material, the Open Shelter Home programme is presented as more than a place of temporary safety. It brings together daily essentials, learning, health, emotional support, recreation, vocational exposure, family restoration and coordination with child-protection authorities.

GSSKS describes its work as part of a wider effort to support children and families in Bhopal's gas-affected communities, with dignity, compassion and attention to each child's best interests.
