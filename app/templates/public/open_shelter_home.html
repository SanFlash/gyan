At a Glance

A community-based care programme in Bhopal.

GSSKS's Open Shelter Homes provide day-long care and support for children in need of care and protection (CNCP), including children and adolescents aged 6–17. Four centres serve children and families in the Gas-Affected Area (Chhola) and Pushpa Nagar, near Bhopal Railway Station.

Why Open Shelter Support Matters

A safe place when children and families need support.

GSSKS focuses on children facing vulnerable circumstances, including children involved in begging or rag-picking, working children, children found in vulnerable settlements or on railway platforms, and children whose parents work as daily-wage labourers with uncertain working hours.

01

Children in vulnerable situations

Children may face unsafe street conditions, exploitative work, begging, rag-picking and other difficult circumstances.

02

Support for working families

Parents engaged in daily-wage work may have unpredictable return times. Open Shelter Homes provide a safe and supportive place for children while their parents are at work.

03

Care with dignity

The programme brings together essential care, learning, health, emotional support, recreation and family reintegration.

Programme Framework

Operating within the child-protection framework.

GSSKS's Open Shelter Home programme operates within the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 and Central Rules, 2016, within the Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS), now under Mission Vatsalya. It identifies the Department of Women and Child Development, Government of Madhya Pradesh, as the supervising department.

Facilities & Services

Care that supports everyday life and development.

Our services combine practical daily care with education, health, emotional support, recreation and rehabilitation follow-up.

Safe shelter & accommodation

A secure environment with age-appropriate arrangements. Facilities include fire-safety measures, television, refrigerator, water purifiers and kitchen essentials.

Nutrition & daily essentials

Balanced breakfast, lunch and dinner are provided alongside tea and snacks, clothing, footwear, toiletries such as soap and oil, and essential textbooks and study materials.

Education & learning support

Government-school admission support, basic literacy, non-formal bridge education, remedial classes, tuition or study assistance, textbooks and digital/computer learning.

Health & physical wellbeing

Routine medical check-ups, immunisation, first-aid support, physical fitness, sports and health-related guidance. Regular medical check-ups and first-aid support are part of the programme.

Counselling & life skills

Individual and group counselling, emotional support, trauma care, behaviour management, value education, anger and stress management, and life-skills learning.

Juvenile justice support

Assistance with Child Welfare Committee (CWC) and Juvenile Justice Board (JJB) procedures, Individual Care Plans (ICPs), inspections and rehabilitation follow-up.

Skill & vocational development

For adolescents, particularly those aged 14–17, the programme includes basic computer training, crafts or trade skills, career guidance and pre-employment training.

Recreation & personality development

Indoor and outdoor games, sports, arts, music, cultural activities, plantation, festivals, national days, birthdays, picnics and creative activities.

Family & social reintegration

Family counselling, restoration efforts and follow-up after restoration are included in the programme description.

Outreach, awareness & monitoring

Field visits to identify children in need, child-rights awareness, case files, progress reports and regular reporting to the Department of Women and Child Development.

Annual Report Archive

Three years of activities recorded by GSSKS.

GSSKS's programme records highlight activities carried out across three reporting years. These snapshots reflect the activities and initiatives of each period.

REPORTING YEAR · 2022–23

Daily care, health and learning

  • Routine medical check-ups and first-aid kits provided.
  • Three meals a day reported according to a diet plan.
  • Vaccination reported for students aged 12–14 years.
  • Indoor and outdoor games, with prizes for children participating in games and cultural activities.
  • Uniforms and stationery distributed to children.
  • Digital learning and computer classes provided.
  • Official and child-welfare visits and interactions.
  • Uniforms and books distributed by representatives associated with the Women and Child Development Department.
REPORTING YEAR · 2023–24

Learning, exposure and participation

  • Daily routine beginning with morning prayer and three healthy meals.
  • Educational visit to Rani Kamalapati Palace on 19 July 2023.
  • World Environment Day activities on 5 June, including planting trees and learning about nature.
  • Independence Day celebration on 15 August 2023.
  • Visit by the Chairperson and members of the Child Welfare Committee; uniforms, notebooks and learning materials distributed.
  • Gandhi Jayanti activities and distribution of educational supplies.
  • Children's Day and Christmas activities, including a picnic to People's Mall on 25 December 2023.
  • Medical check-ups, first aid, indoor/outdoor games, computer learning and annual examinations.
  • Legal-literacy interaction/camp during an inspection by a District Legal Services Authority officer, recorded as 16 May 2023.
REPORTING YEAR · 2024–25

Skills, wellbeing and community activities

  • Three healthy meals a day and routine medical check-ups.
  • Employment-oriented training provided to children.
  • Painting, rangoli and other creative activities.
  • International Yoga Day celebrated with children.
  • Diwali celebrations and cleanliness drives among shelter-home students.
  • Visit by SJPU In-charge Inspectors Kiran and Aarti.
  • Discussion with Dr. Rahul Sharma, clinical psychologist at J.P. Hospital, during a centre visit.
  • Computer classes, annual examinations, picnics, plantation and cultural activities alongside other learning, recreation and community activities.
Capacity & Reporting Context

Figures must be read alongside the year they describe.

Programme capacity figures have varied across reporting periods. They are shown with their respective years and should not be read as current occupancy.

2022–23 REPORT269

Combined capacity stated for two units

The 2022–23 programme record lists Unit 1 capacity as 199 and Unit 2 capacity as 71, totalling 269. This is a historical figure and differs from the four-unit capacity recorded in the following year.

2023–24 REPORT100

Combined capacity stated for four units

The 2023–24 programme record lists 25 places per unit across four units (4 × 25 = 100), following recognition of four Open Shelter Home units.

ORGANISATIONAL REACH~1,200

Children reportedly integrated over several years

GSSKS reports that approximately 1,200 children have been integrated into the social mainstream over several years. This is a cumulative organisational estimate; the reporting period and calculation method are not specified.

Important interpretation269 and 100 are historical capacity figures printed in different annual-report contexts, not the number of children currently living at the centres. Approximately 1,200 is an organisation-reported cumulative figure, not an annual total. For formal reporting, GSSKS should use the latest sanctioned capacity and current operating details for each centre.
Programme Commitment

Protection, care and opportunity—one child at a time.

The Open Shelter Home programme is more than a place of temporary safety. It brings together daily essentials, learning, health, emotional support, recreation, vocational exposure, family restoration and coordination with child-protection authorities.

GSSKS's work is part of a wider effort to support children and families in Bhopal's gas-affected communities, with dignity, compassion and attention to each child's best interests.
